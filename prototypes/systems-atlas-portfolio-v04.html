02 / ApproachHow I think about technical work
The method

Explore widely.
Constrain deliberately.

Complexity is easy to introduce and expensive to maintain. The challenge is to make good decisions early, then keep questioning those decisions against real evidence.

I.

Define the boundary.

Understand the business problem, the surrounding systems and the responsibilities the solution should not inherit.

II.

Make the trade-off.

Prefer understandable decisions over technological novelty, and use working vertical slices to test assumptions.

III.

Prove the behaviour.

Separate implementation, tests, hosted verification and actual business impact. Evidence only means what it can support.

03 / Exhibit oneFor The Table · Software & operations
From hospitality problem to shared SaaS

For
The Table.

Making ordering more convenient for customers can easily create more work for the venue. The engineering challenge was to design a lightweight layer that fits around existing operations, while still supporting many independent businesses on shared infrastructure.

The decisive constraint

One ordinary staff action.

Staff confirm the order. The venue's existing till and payment process remain authoritative. The product deliberately avoids becoming another EPOS or an elaborate kitchen workflow engine.

Beneath the experience
Three boundaries worth understanding.
Illustrative model
I / Identity

Venue context

A guest session is tied to a venue and table. A table number is context, not proof of identity or physical presence.

II / Authority

Tenant isolation

Staff and operational mutations are constrained by venue membership and server-side authorisation boundaries.

III / Operation

Order state

A submission is not a commitment. Staff confirmation is the normal point at which a venue accepts an order.

A submitted order enters a venue-scoped staff queue. The venue confirms it, while ordinary payment stays outside FTT.
Three defining decisions

The architecture follows the constraints.

Decision 01 / Operational restraint

Less software for the venue to operate.

A venue-level QR, customer-selected table and short-lived anonymous session keep the entry journey simple. The staff workflow has one routine action: confirm the order. More elaborate verification and fulfilment requirements were rejected when they threatened the central value proposition.

Decision 02 / Multi-tenancy

One platform without shared authority.

Independent venues operate on the same application, API and PostgreSQL infrastructure. The important engineering work is not drawing multiple venues in a diagram; it is binding identity, membership, order state and administrative mutations to the correct venue, then exercising those boundaries in real database tests.

Decision 03 / Release engineering

Built is not the same as launched.

Security reviews, isolated backup and point-in-time restore drills, sandbox subscription acceptance and a hosted synthetic operational rehearsal tested what happens beyond the happy path. These records demonstrate meaningful technical readiness without establishing live commercial billing, customer traction or final external launch approval.

Verified in project records

End-to-end rehearsal

The 29 September 2026 readiness board records a hosted synthetic exercise including onboarding, order flow, repeat ordering, suspension and reinstatement.

Design decision

Less integration, less burden

The initial product excludes ordinary customer payment processing and leaves the existing venue system authoritative. An earlier OTP requirement was deliberately superseded.

Not established

Commercial traction

Technical acceptance does not mean paying customers or commercial launch. External launch and operational gates remain. Private source repository ↗
