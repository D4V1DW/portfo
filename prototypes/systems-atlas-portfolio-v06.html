02 / ApproachHow I think about technical work
The method

Explore widely.
Constrain deliberately.

Complexity is easy to introduce and expensive to maintain. The challenge is to make good decisions early, then keep questioning those decisions against real evidence.

I.

Define the boundary.

Understand the business problem, the surrounding systems and the responsibilities the solution should not inherit.

II.

Make the trade-off.

Prefer understandable decisions over technological novelty, and use working vertical slices to test assumptions.

III.

Prove the behaviour.

Separate implementation, tests, hosted verification and actual business impact. Evidence only means what it can support.

Making ordering more convenient for customers can easily create more work for the venue. The engineering challenge was to design a lightweight layer that fits around existing operations, while still supporting many independent businesses on shared infrastructure.

The decisive constraint

One ordinary staff action.

Staff confirm the order. The venue's existing till and payment process remain authoritative. The product deliberately avoids becoming another EPOS or an elaborate kitchen workflow engine.

Beneath the experience
Three boundaries worth understanding.
Illustrative model
I / Identity

Venue context

A guest session is tied to a venue and table. A table number is context, not proof of identity or physical presence.

II / Authority

Tenant isolation

Staff and operational mutations are constrained by venue membership and server-side authorisation boundaries.

III / Operation

Order state

A submission is not a commitment. Staff confirmation is the normal point at which a venue accepts an order.

A submitted order enters a venue-scoped staff queue. The venue confirms it, while ordinary payment stays outside FTT.
Three defining decisions

The architecture follows the constraints.

Decision 01 / Operational restraint

Less software for the venue to operate.

A venue-level QR, customer-selected table and short-lived anonymous session keep the entry journey simple. The staff workflow has one routine action: confirm the order. More elaborate verification and fulfilment requirements were rejected when they threatened the central value proposition.

Decision 02 / Multi-tenancy

One platform without shared authority.

Independent venues operate on the same application, API and PostgreSQL infrastructure. The important engineering work is not drawing multiple venues in a diagram; it is binding identity, membership, order state and administrative mutations to the correct venue, then exercising those boundaries in real database tests.

Decision 03 / Release engineering

Built is not the same as launched.

Security reviews, isolated backup and point-in-time restore drills, sandbox subscription acceptance and a hosted synthetic operational rehearsal tested what happens beyond the happy path. These records demonstrate meaningful technical readiness without establishing live commercial billing, customer traction or final external launch approval.

Verified in project records

End-to-end rehearsal

The 29 September 2026 readiness board records a hosted synthetic exercise including onboarding, order flow, repeat ordering, suspension and reinstatement.

Design decision

Less integration, less burden

The initial product excludes ordinary customer payment processing and leaves the existing venue system authoritative. An earlier OTP requirement was deliberately superseded.

Not established

Commercial traction

Technical acceptance does not mean paying customers or commercial launch. External launch and operational gates remain. Private source repository ↗

01 — Intent / The starting point

The conversation
becomes a product.

Before anyone writes a function, the idea needs a shape. This was the brief: a relaxed browser puzzle game built around turning circuit components and watching power move through them.

A brief / distilled from planning records
“Rotate the pieces. Find a path for the power. Let every receiver light up.”
But what does that actually require?

Deterministic rules. Twenty designed levels. Clear progression. An experience that remains usable on a phone.

Decisions that cannot be left to chance
Chosen product rule

All required receivers must be powered. The engine evaluates port-level paths deterministically; a visually plausible connection is not sufficient.

PAF doesn't decide the product by itself. Its planning process preserves the human-approved choices so later agents don't need to reconstruct the original conversation.

05 — Judgment / The review room

Confidence is not
evidence.

Explore two recorded review episodes. Compare the finding with the accepted contract or observable defect before revealing what the evidence supported.

Finding / accepted contract
A disputed branching mechanic.

The reviewer wanted Level 6 redesigned because it reused branching introduced earlier. The design contract deliberately allowed the progression.

Evidence statusInspect the contract →
REVIEWER CLAIM
Reusing a tee is a design problem.

The reviewer prefers a different mechanic instead of the agreed progression. A separate inaccurate location in level instructions is a genuine copy error.

Review assertions are hypotheses until tested against an accepted source or a reproducible failure.
What the framework learned

A reviewer must identify a contract violation or a concrete counterexample. A design preference is not a blocking defect. Significant repairs also need evidence on the final changed revision, not just a passing suite on an earlier head.

Before → After / PAF evolution
Skills-first, project-owned.

Keep valuable fail-closed checks, but rely on repository contracts and the host's native coordination. Load relevant skills and engineering knowledge only where they add useful context.

No extra PAF daemon. No fabricated trusted approval.
Experienced beyond the pilot

PAF's planning contracts and staged build approach are used on other personal projects. The practical value is substantial to the practitioner, even though a controlled throughput or cost benchmark is not available.

Evidence boundary

Circuit Lab is one documented end-to-end run, not a multi-project proof of causal uplift. Native Cursor performed the coordination. The value proposition is an engineering method, not an assertion that the framework wrote the game unaided.

01 / Intent
A deliberately different engineering exhibit

A framework worth using is one that makes the work easier.

PAF is valuable because it helps preserve decisions and continuity through complex, AI-assisted implementation. The strongest evidence for the method is not the number of files in the framework. It's the way project contracts, running agents, review and acceptance can cooperate without losing sight of the actual product.

This is an interactive reconstruction of recorded private-project evidence, not an active autonomous build, a simulation of agent internals or a live benchmark.
